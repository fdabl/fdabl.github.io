Hi there, I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Psychological and Behavioural Science and the Global School of Sustainability at the [London School of Economics and Political Science](https://www.lse.ac.uk/people/fabian-dablander).

The IPCC has warned that “there is a rapidly closing window of opportunity to secure a liveable and sustainable future for all.” I aim to contribute to securing such a future through research, teaching, and community building.

My research focuses on understanding how climate action and wider societal transformation can be accelerated. Combining behavioural science, data science, and complex-systems approaches, I study topics including sufficiency and limits, media coverage of climate change, the effects of extreme weather events, the role of scientists on a planet in crisis, and the drivers of climate engagement among elites and the wider public. I am a founding member of the Climate Media Lab and am working to build a behavioural data observatory that integrates large-scale behavioural and psychological data to support evidence-based policymaking.

In my teaching, I aim to give students a rigorous understanding of the behavioural and societal changes needed to address the climate and ecological crisis, and a clearer sense of their own agency in bringing those changes about. I have taught on the Complex Systems and Policy Master's programme at the University of Amsterdam and delivered guest lectures on climate action across a range of courses. At LSE, I am developing a new course on climate and societal change, planned for Autumn 2027.

Societal change requires building communities of action and strengthening connections with practitioners and civil society. I have co-founded the Amsterdam Complexity School on Climate Change and have co-organised it annually since 2024, bringing together researchers and non-academic stakeholders to work on collaborative projects for climate action. I was also involved in Scientist Rebellion, where I helped organise national and international initiatives and build a global network of engaged scholars. I co-founded CorrelAid Netherlands, which connects data scientists with organisations working for social impact, and was a Data Science for Social Good Fellow at Carnegie Mellon University in 2022.

Before joining LSE, I have held research positions at several institutes at the University of Amsterdam, including the interdisciplinary climate institute SEVEN and the Institute for Biodiversity and Ecosystem Dynamics. I received my PhD in Psychological Methods from the University of Amsterdam, with research spanning Bayesian hypothesis testing, causal inference from observational data, and early warning signals of tipping points.
